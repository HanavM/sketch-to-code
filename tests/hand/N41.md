# n41: the first build keeps to the words people typed, and never stacks words on words

Round 32 c2 (roastery, n39 try 2): the heading "Small batch, roasted on Thursdays." was drawn as three level
lines; the note took two. The third line, longer than the second, was read as a new block, and the first
build put copy nobody typed on it ("Fresh-roasted, thoughtfully sourced"), right on top of the heading.
Nothing checked a first build for that: the fix check (n37) only looks at overlaps a fix creates.

## What was built
- **Words on words and words nobody typed are checked on every build** (packages/pipeline/src/ownwords.ts,
  in the build's page checks): two pieces of text (neither inside the other) overlapping by a quarter of the
  smaller one fail; a new piece of two or more words, under a third of them typed anywhere, fails when it runs
  into other text or sits on no drawn mark. The repair is told which words to take out, or which two to part.
  Untyped words on a drawn line with room of their own are left alone (a bare line still says "text here").
- **The brief says it too**: a level line with no note right under lines whose note has more words than they
  hold is that text's next line; never words nobody typed on it, never two texts on top of each other.
- **A heading's third line may be longer than its second** (packages/ink/src/textmark.ts): lines now only
  have to be no longer than the first line, so "Small batch, / roasted on / Thursdays." is one 3-line heading,
  shown on the sketch as "on these 3 lines" before the build. A longer line under a 3-line heading is its
  subtitle, not the 4th row of a ruled list.
- Unit tests: the roastery geometry for both (invented line caught; clean build passes; overlaps; untyped words
  on a free drawn line allowed; overlaps that were already there ignored; 3-line stack with the subtitle out).
