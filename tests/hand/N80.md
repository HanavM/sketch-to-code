# n80: rows are quick to give

Round 35 (n76/n77) kept strokes low (13-18 a page) but words ran 120-139 on four of five app pages: every row
of a list or table was typed out as its own note, and a card note with dot separators was printed as one line.

## What was built (`rowdata.ts`, zero tokens)
- **Cells:** a note's parts split by `·`, `•` or `|` are separate text items: name first, details next, side
  value last; the separators are never printed. A dotted row's last part ends at a comma (", teal Refill" is a
  look, not copy). Checked: no single piece of text carries two cells of a row.
- **Rows as data:** a note with rows split by ` / ` (or new lines), with or without a leading "5 rows:", fills
  the drawn rows in order. Checked: every typed row is on the page, top to bottom in the typed order.
- **Sample rows:** "6 rows like this", "5 more like this", "4 like this", "x6" or "×6" after one typed row
  makes that many rows in that row's shape, the rest filled with made-up sample values of the same kind. Those
  words are allowed (the "words nobody typed" check only judges them if they run into other text). Checked:
  that many rows start at the first row's left edge with its look; if the rows exist but lack the first row's
  parts (its circle, its button), the repair is told to give every row every part.
- `replay.mjs --notes <file>`: replay a page's strokes and taps with the notes typed the short way.
