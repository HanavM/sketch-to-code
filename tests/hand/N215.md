# n215: taps in a line inside a box are small dots, never a fill

Found in n211 (round 46 e2, team roster, 88.9 only after a fix): a table outline, two ruled lines, and a green, an amber
and a grey tap down the status column. Tap means "fill what it lands in", so the lines made bands: first row bright
green, the rest of the table grey. It took an 11-word fix note.

## The lead was too broad, so the rule changed
The lead said "a tap in a colour other than the box's pen is a dot". A count over 170 earlier sketches says no: 175 boxes
have one off-colour tap, and that is the normal fill gesture (a pale fill in a darker outline). So the rule is narrower:
**three or more taps, in at least two colours, in a straight column or row, in a box holding other drawn parts.** Of
those 170 sketches only this page has that. Each tap in its own ruled band stays painted rows (n91); a divider with two
taps stays two fills (n59); three colours in an empty box stay a fade.

## What was built (zero tokens)
- **The reading** (`inkfill.ts`, `tapDots`): those taps leave the fills, so the overlay stops painting the box the moment
  the third tap lands, and its status line says it is a small dot.
- **Told and checked** (`tapdots.ts`): each dot at its spot in its colour, on every row, a row with no tap taking the
  colour of a tapped row with the same word, the word after the dot in its colour. The check fails on a missing dot or a
  big block painted a dot's colour.
- **One tint a mark** (overlay): five notes on one table stacked five amber tints, so the table looked painted amber
  while sketching. Now one tint.
