# n203: a size word on a character inside a small drawn box means as big as fits comfortably; the boxes stay visible

Found in n201: the two-factor code page kept its six drawn boxes and put 4, 8, 1 inside them, but the digits came
out about twice the target's size (86.5 / 88.6, below the 92.0 the fixed page reached).

## Why it happened
- "4, 8, 1 big bold in first three boxes" was read twice. The n201 rule put the digits in the boxes, but the older
  big-word rule (a box with a "big" note is the area a big word fills) also fired on the first box: its checks asked
  for a 101px "4" filling the box and for the box's border to go. Two rules pulling against each other; whenever the
  repair round followed the big-word one, the digits blew up.
- Nothing said what "big" means for a character inside a small box, so the build guessed large.

## What was built (zero tokens)
- **A note that lists single characters for a row of drawn boxes is never a big word's area** (`areas.ts` skips it;
  `isBoxCharsNote` in `repeatmark.ts`). The conflicting checks no longer fire.
- **"Big" inside a small box is told as a size**: about 45% of the box's side (32px in a 72px box), never above
  58%, bold kept, with room round it. **Checked on the page**: a character larger than that goes to the repair
  round with the size to use.
- **The drawn boxes keep a visible outline**: told (1-2px grey border, never near-white or only a faint fill) and
  checked (every drawn box needs a border a person can see).
- Tests: `packages/pipeline/tests/n203-box-char-size.test.ts` (6).

## Did it help
- **Yes.** The digits now match the target's size in every try, and with the conflicting big-word reading gone the
  page lands at 90.9 and 89.6 with no fix (was 86.5 / 88.6 with none, 92.0 only with a fix), in about 3 minutes and fewer
  tokens. 11 fewer words than the hand sketch needed.
- Left on this page: the bold left-panel line ("Your account, locked tight.", big bold) is built small on one line,
  and the short pen line drawn under it is pasted as a white rule. That is the open "pen line under a heading is
  where its words go, never a bar" lead (n199).
