# n59: a divider with a colour tapped on each side is two fills; a line drawn for words is never a rule

Replayed the n49 sketch of round 33 c3 (gig ticket) word for word with everything through n57 plus this step:
first build only, no fix notes, no note drags.

## The gaps (from n57)
- **A straight divider across a box with a white tap on one side and a pink tap on the other was read as one
  white-to-pink fade over the whole ticket.** Only a curve (n51) cut a box; a straight line was never a split.
- **Lines drawn for words came back as visible rules** under the date and on the stub, as well as the words.

## What was built
- **Two fills at a drawn divider** (`@s2c/ink` `dividerSplit`, `splitCss`; `markFill` takes the strokes): a nearly
  straight line running across a closed mark, edge to edge, with a different colour tapped on each side, makes two
  solid fills meeting at that line. The overlay paints each side in its colour the moment the pen lifts ("filled
  this side of the line drawn across it"). The build is told the exact hard-stop gradient in the block's own frame
  (it stays right when the box is drawn turned) or two solid parts meeting at the line, and the page is checked
  (`inkcolour.ts` `splitVerdict`): a hard stop, or one solid part on each side; a fade fails.
- **A line drawn for words is only where the words sit** (`textrules.ts`): a line with its own note that carries
  copy (also a slanted line on a turned ticket) must not come back as an hr, a thin bar along it, or an underline
  under its words; "dashed", "divider" and the like still make a rule. Told in the brief, checked on the page. The
  page snapshot now records bottom borders and keeps gradients up to 240 characters (hard stops were cut at 80).
