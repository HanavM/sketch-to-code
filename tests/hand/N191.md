# n191: the plan names each part by its look, and a tap picks the part a fix is about

Found in n188 and n190 (round 44 e5 delivery tracker, e3 photo editor): a whole-sketch fix named a part
loosely ("grey track", "sky"), the reading aimed it at the wrong element, and the plan said only
`MODIFY e9 · <path>`, so the wrong pick sat in plain sight until ✓ Go changed the wrong thing.

## What was built
- **Parts named by their look.** A plan target with no words of its own is named by colour and shape from its
  own painted style ("orange line", "light grey bar", "peach circle", "dark dot") instead of its tag
  (`overlay-client/src/target-look.ts`). Each part an action points at gets its own line on the plan card.
- **"not this", then tap.** Every part on the plan card has a "not this" button; the next press on the page
  picks the stamped element under it, the outline moves there at once, and the card says what was picked.
  Tapping the same spot again picks the part around the last pick (added after the avatar tap below landed on
  its tiny icon; built and type-checked, not yet used live).
- **The pick reaches the build.** ✓ Go sends the picks; the server swaps the tapped element (found by its
  source stamp, nearest the tapped box) into that action before grounding, tells the build "the person tapped
  this, leave the old one exactly as it is", and writes `retargets.json` in the run record
  (`pipeline/src/retarget.ts`).
- Hand bench: `session.mjs` op `run {hold:true}` stops at the plan and returns its rows; `pick {action, from,
  x, y}` presses "not this" and taps; `go` presses Go. Picks count in the digest. `replay.mjs --hold-fix`.
- Tests: `pipeline/tests/n191-retarget.test.ts` (6), `overlay-client/tests/n191-target-look.test.ts` (4).

## Did it help
- Yes on e5: the fix changed the parts meant and nothing else; 66.5 → 70.6 (47.0 in n188), with the same
  sketch and the same fix words. The wrong picks were readable on the card before Go ("orange line" for a grey
  track), which is the whole point.
- Cost: three taps, no extra words. A first tap can land on a small inner part; the same-spot re-tap now climbs.
