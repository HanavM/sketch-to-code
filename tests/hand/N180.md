# n180: a one-line heading sits on and along its drawn line

Found in n179 (round 43 e4 pet adoption, replay): "18 dogs near you" was built 15-30px under its drawn line and
40-60px right of where the line starts, and every check called it fine.

## What the run records showed
- The line check passed any text the line merely touched ("runs through it"): letters whose top sat on the line passed.
- Once that was tightened, the heading came out centred and short. The brief itself said so: a short line over the
  first card (330-560 over a card at 329-613) was read as "that card's label, CENTRED on x 471". A line that starts on
  the card's left edge is flush with it, not centred over it.
- Every pet run also failed "paint its text exactly #ececec" for the header's pale full-width rule (no words on it), a
  check no build could pass, so one repair per run was spent on it.

## What was built
- `verify.ts` (line placement): for one line of words, the drawn line is the letters' middle. More than about a third
  of the line height off fails, saying up or down and how far. One line of words alone on a drawn line must run along
  it (both ends within 12% of the line); a row of nav links on one line is not held to this.
- `textplace.ts` `columnOf`: a line clearly shorter than a mark that starts or ends on the mark's edge is flush with
  it, never that mark's centred label.
- `ruleline.ts` `markPaleDividers`: a pale line across most of the page with no words is a divider; its pen colours
  the rule only (the check now asks only that no words are painted that pale colour).
- Tests: `packages/pipeline/tests/n180-heading-on-line.test.ts` (10, on the real pet heading, nav, rule and card).
