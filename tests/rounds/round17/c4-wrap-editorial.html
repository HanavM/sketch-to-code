Gardens that remember the rain

Long before anyone measured rainfall, the people who farmed these terraces built walls that held water through the dry months. Some of those walls are eight hundred years old, and they still work. Engineers have started coming to study them, notebooks out, asking the same question the farmers answered with stone: how do you keep a summer from wasting a winter? The answer, it turns out, is patience, and a lot of moss. Every terrace drains into the next one, slowly, so nothing runs off and nothing floods. It is a design that only makes sense if you plan for your grandchildren.
